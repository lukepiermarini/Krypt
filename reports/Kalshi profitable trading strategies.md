# Patient limit orders on favorites beat everything else

The best-supported way for a small, automated beginner to make money on Kalshi is boring. Rest limit orders that buy the likely outcome at roughly 72 to 92 cents, in markets where makers pay no fee, and never buy cheap longshots. Start with daily weather temperature markets. The largest study of Kalshi trades finds that contracts under 10 cents lose more than 60% of the money put into them. Contracts above 70 cents earn small but statistically significant gains after fees. People who post resting orders ("makers") lose far less than people who cross the spread ("takers"): about −10% against −31% on average. Makers buying at 50 cents or more earned about **+2.6% per contract**. That edge is thin. It is noisy (a standard deviation of 33%), it may be shrinking, and it was measured before Kalshi started charging some makers fees. You should expect a few percent per trade, not a money machine, and you will need roughly a thousand fills to prove the edge is real. The more glamorous ideas are mostly taken or unproven: 15-minute crypto bots, copying sharp sportsbook lines, trading economic data against consensus, cross-venue arbitrage, and pure AI forecasting. Your first test should be the "patient favorites" strategy in weather markets. The runner-up is an AI forecasting agent on slow, rules-heavy markets with deliberately small bets, run on paper alongside the first test.

## A beginner's map: what a Kalshi price means and who wins

A Kalshi contract pays $1 if its event happens and $0 if it doesn't, so a price of 85 cents roughly means the market thinks the outcome is 85% likely. You make money only when the true chance is higher than the price you paid, plus fees. There are two ways to trade. A **taker** crosses the spread and fills right away against someone else's resting order. A **maker** posts a limit order that waits in the book until someone else hits it. Krypt Trader's limit orders let you be a maker, which matters because the evidence below is overwhelmingly about that distinction.

Bürgi, Deng and Whelan studied Kalshi trade data from its 2021 launch through April 2025. They covered 46,282 contracts across 12,403 events and 313,972 daily prices, and found a strong **favorite-longshot bias**: cheap contracts are systematically overpriced and expensive ones slightly underpriced ([Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Their worked example shows the size of it. A 5-cent contract that wins 3% of the time returns −40% before fees, while a 95-cent contract that wins 98% of the time returns +3.1%. The average taker return was **−31.46%** and the average maker return **−9.64%**. Makers buying at 50 cents or more made **+2.6%** on average ([Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). The bias showed up in every price horizon from 0 to 10 days before close, in every volume and trade-size bucket, and in most categories. Higher-volume markets were not meaningfully more efficient. Two details matter for strategy design. First, the pattern was weakest in politics and entertainment, where it was not statistically significant. Second, it was weakest in the 2025 data, which the authors read as "some evidence that the bias in prices is diminishing over time" ([Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Independent work on Polymarket finds the same overpriced longshots and underpriced favorites ([Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/d5708372-5ea3-4252-b669-7f9cee07387c)). A 353-million-trade study across both venues finds that calibration depends on the category, with political prices squeezed toward 50% ([Le 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)).

The authors also explain why nobody has arbitraged this away. The order books are thin: the April 2025 CPI market had only **$33.60** available at the best ask. The risk per trade is high, and few traders knew about the pattern ([Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). That thinness is good news for a small bankroll. A $500 account can trade in markets too small for a professional firm to bother with.

The base rate for participants is sobering. A separate study reported in the press finds takers lose about **1.12% per trade** to makers, and the Roosevelt Institute estimates takers lost **$584M** between July 2021 and mid-May 2026. Kalshi disputes that framing ([American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/); [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/kalshi-rebuffs-think-tank-claim-regular-traders-lost-584-million-on-platform/)). Kalshi's own one-month snapshot shows about **26% of users** in profit. A professional gambler who polled traders and employees puts the long-run profitable share at **2–5%**, and points out that buying fair 50/50 contracts as a taker loses about 3.4% after fees, which is worse than single-zero roulette ([How Gambling Works](https://howgamblingworks.substack.com/p/are-30-of-kalshi-users-really-profitable)). The maker-side profit increasingly goes to institutional firms such as Susquehanna, which get fee discounts and rebates ([American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/08/26/house-always-wins-kalshi-prediction-markets/)). Your bot will be competing with them, so it needs to go where they don't.

## Fees decide which markets are worth touching

Kalshi charges takers **ceil(0.07 × contracts × P × (1−P))**, rounded up to the next cent. Makers pay **ceil(0.0175 × C × P × (1−P))**, but only on series flagged for maker fees. There is no settlement fee, and cancelling a resting order is free ([Kalshi Fee Schedule PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20260612075630/https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). Fees are largest at 50 cents and shrink toward the extremes. That is one more reason favorites are a cheaper place to trade.

| Price | Taker fee per contract | Taker fee as % of stake | Taker needs true win chance above | Maker fee (only on maker-fee series) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10¢ | 0.63¢ | 6.3% | 10.6% | 0.16¢ |
| 50¢ | 1.75¢ | 3.5% | 51.75% | 0.44¢ |
| 70¢ | 1.47¢ | 2.1% | 71.5% | 0.37¢ |
| 85¢ | 0.89¢ | 1.05% | 85.9% | 0.22¢ |
| 90¢ | 0.63¢ | 0.7% | 90.6% | 0.16¢ |
| 95¢ | 0.33¢ | 0.35% | 95.3% | 0.08¢ |

*Large-lot figures computed from the official formula ([Kalshi Fee Schedule PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20260612075630/https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)).*

The most useful fact for this bot comes from a live API check on October 8, 2026. **Weather series (such as KXHIGHNY), the 15-minute crypto series and the S&P/Nasdaq series charge takers only. Makers there pay nothing.** The flagship game-winner series (NBA, NFL, NHL, college football, tennis, EPL) and the main macro series (Fed, CPI, payrolls, GDP) do charge makers. MLB was halved to a 0.5 multiplier in August 2026. All 419 Climate & Weather series were taker-only ([Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/fee_changes?show_historical=true)). The schedule changes often. The fee-change log has 107 entries between October 2025 and September 2026, and on July 3, 2026 the old half-rate for S&P and Nasdaq ended, so guides that still quote 0.035 are out of date ([Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/fee_changes?show_historical=true)). Your bot should read `fee_type` and `fee_multiplier` from each series instead of assuming them.

Rounding hurts small orders. One contract at 50 cents pays 2 cents in taker fee, which is 4% instead of 3.5%. One contract at 95 cents pays a full cent, about three times the large-order rate ([Kalshi Fee Schedule PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20260612075630/https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). Exiting before settlement pays the fee a second time, so a taker round trip at 50 cents costs about 3.5 cents. For a small edge, holding to settlement as a maker is the only arithmetic that works. Idle cash and open positions also earn about **3.25% APY** on balances of $250 or more ([Kalshi Help – APY](https://help.kalshi.com/navigating-the-exchange/your-portfolio/apy-on-kalshi)), which roughly offsets having capital tied up in resting orders.

## Most famous bot edges are already competed away

The 15-minute crypto markets look made for a bot, and that is the problem. KXBTC15M settles on a 60-second average of the CF Benchmarks Bitcoin index ([PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/kalshi-bitcoin-15-minute-markets)). A study of 2,764 windows in August and September 2026 found every 10-cent price band within about one point of its actual win rate, including the last three minutes. Contracts priced at 46.2 cents won 45.8% of the time, and 91.9 cents won 91.4%. Even favorites at 95–99 cents with 45 seconds left returned about their price minus fees ([PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/articles/is-kalshi-bitcoin-rigged)). The books are professionally quoted, with a median spread of **0.82 cents** and about **3,300 contracts** sitting at the best price on each side ([Cryptostruct](https://cryptostruct.com/guides/kalshi-vs-polymarket-data)). A new resting order from your bot would wait behind thousands of contracts and get filled mostly when the price is about to move against it. TurbineFi backtested thousands of KXBTC15M strategies. With no fees and easy fills, 69.5% looked profitable. With fees, depth limits and realistic next-candle fills, only **2.1%** were profitable. The best archetype from the first run then had **7 of 4,290 variants** profitable nine days later ([TurbineFi](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/do-automated-trading-bots-make-money-prediction-markets-2026)). On Polymarket, latency arbitrage was killed on purpose with fees that peak at 50 cents ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/)). A bot on home internet loses those speed races to servers advertising about 1ms to Kalshi ([NYC Servers](https://newyorkcityservers.com/kalshi-vps)). The 38 crypto presets in Krypt Trader are best used as a control group, and that use is covered later.

Sports and economic data look like information edges but behave like efficient markets. Sports was about 68% of Kalshi volume in March 2026 ([KuCoin/PANews summary of Blockworks](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/kalshi-s-sports-prediction-market-volume-surges-80x-in-one-year)). Kalshi's NFL pricing was as tight as FanDuel's and DraftKings' in a Week 1 2026 sample ([Prediction News](https://predictionnews.com/story/kalshi-vs-the-sportsbooks-nfl-game-prices-week-by-week)), and vendors sell Kalshi-versus-Pinnacle feeds, which means that strategy is a commodity ([SharpAPI](https://sharpapi.io/sportsbooks/kalshi-odds-api)). The flagship sports series charge maker fees, and sports carries legal risk. A circuit split is headed for a Supreme Court petition, and Michigan forced trades to be unwound in June 2026 ([CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/08/39-states-urge-supreme-court-to-review-kalshi-sports-betting-dispute/); [SailGP](https://sailgp.com/prediction-markets/kalshi/pennsylvania)). For economic data, a Federal Reserve/NBER paper finds Kalshi's headline CPI forecast is significantly *better* than Bloomberg consensus ([NBER w34702](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34702); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/113897/fed-study-kalshi-economic-forecasting)). A bot that trades consensus against Kalshi would be betting against the better forecaster.

Cross-venue arbitrage is real but closed to a small US beginner. Equivalent markets on different venues do drift **2–4%** apart for long stretches. But only about 6% of events are listed on more than one venue. The impressive yearly returns come from short times to resolution, and a 1,218% backtest rested on just **15 completed trades** in 800 days ([Gebele & Matthes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01706)). Settlement rules often differ between venues. In one example, Kalshi's Fed contract needed a cut of 25bp or more while the Polymarket version paid on any cut ([pm.wiki](https://pm.wiki/es/learn/prediction-market-arbitrage-after-fees)). That turns a "risk-free" arbitrage into a directional bet. Polymarket's main platform was still closed to US users as of April 2026 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-cftc-approval-main-platform-us-report)). Krypt Trader can read Polymarket prices as a signal, but cannot trade both legs.

### AI forecasters match the market but lose money trading it

By July 2026 frontier AI forecasters had "likely reached parity" with superforecasters on ForecastBench ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)). Matching the Kalshi price is a different claim from beating it. On 1,367 Kalshi events, GPT-5's Brier score was 0.184 against the market's 0.187, a statistical tie. No model's average return reached break-even: the best returned 0.943 per $1 ([Prophet Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v1)). With real money, six frontier models each given $10,000 lost **16–31% on Kalshi** over 57 days, and 71–97% of their positions were in weather markets ([Prediction Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1)). Models are dangerously overconfident at the extremes. They were wrong 27% of the time when claiming more than 90% confidence ([KalshiBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16030v1)). The one bright spot is that bet sizing turned out to matter as much as accuracy. Sizing bets with a proper-scoring rule produced **+22.1%** in a backtest where Kelly sizing lost 99.9%. A live Gemini 3 agent grew **$200 to $361** over 26 trading days. That is one small run, though, and a co-author is affiliated with Kalshi Research ([arXiv 2607.06166](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06166v1)). AI models did relatively better at long lead times and worse near resolution, where fast news wins ([Prophet Arena](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v1)). AI agents are the wrong tool for 15-minute crypto and a risky tool for directional weather calls, but a plausible tool for slow markets where reading the rules matters.

### Liquidity rewards help, but not on a $500 account

Kalshi's Liquidity Incentive Program pays daily pools of **$1 to $1,000 per market**, scored by random once-per-second snapshots of resting orders on both sides near the best price. The program runs to January 1, 2027 ([CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf)). A snapshot only counts when the whole book reaches a target size of at least 100 contracts on *both* sides. Since July 30, 2026, Kalshi's own affiliates and contracted market makers appear to compete for the same pools ([CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf)). A small bot earns a slice of other people's depth, something like $2.50 a day for a 5% share of a $50 pool. It has to quote both sides through news, which exposes it to being picked off when prices jump, and Basic API accounts get only about 10 order writes per second ([Kalshi rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)). No retail market maker has published verified profits ([Encyclopedia Autonomica](https://jdsemrau.substack.com/p/automated-market-making-on-kalshi); [Bot for Kalshi](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/open-source-kalshi-bot-ecosystem)). Krypt Trader's paper mode also cannot score these rewards, so this one is for later.

## Test first: patient favorites in weather markets

The case for starting here is that five independent facts point the same way. Contracts above 70 cents earned significant post-fee gains. Makers buying at 50 cents or more earned +2.6%. Weather showed a significant favorite-longshot bias, and Kalshi's weather books are small enough that institutions largely ignore them ([Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf); [Bot for Kalshi](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/can-you-bet-on-the-weather)). Weather series charge makers **zero** fees ([Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series/fee_changes?show_historical=true)). Daily temperature markets also settle the next morning, so a $500 bankroll recycles every day instead of sitting locked up for months. One caveat on the evidence: the two research notes disagree on whether weather or crypto showed the steepest bias slope in the Whelan category table. That doesn't change the case, since both were significant. Buying the No side of an overpriced tail bracket is the same trade in another form: a "No" at 88 cents is selling a 12-cent longshot.

Weather is also where beginners lose money by misreading settlement, so build the rules in from day one. Daily highs and lows settle on the **final NWS Daily Climate Report for the specific station** named in the rules, not on any weather app. During Daylight Saving Time, which runs until November 1 this year, the climate "day" runs from **1:00 AM to 12:59 AM local** ([Kalshi Help: Weather Markets](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823837-weather-markets)). **Correction (checked against Kalshi's live series API, Oct 8, 2026):** most current daily temperature series, including KXHIGHNY, KXHIGHCHI, KXHIGHMIA and all the `KXHIGHT…`/`KXLOWT…` cities, now list **The Weather Company** (weather.com/kalshi) as the settlement source for the named station, not the NWS Climate Report. A few older series (e.g. KXDENHIGH, KXHIGHHOU) still cite the NWS Climatological Report. Read each market's `rules_primary` and `settlement_sources` before trading it. A backtest that used LaGuardia observations for a contract that settles on Central Park produced meaningless results ([TurbineFi](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/do-automated-trading-bots-make-money-prediction-markets-2026)). The temperature brackets within one city and day are linked, because only one bracket can win. Treat them as one bet, not six.

Configure a named agent called `Patient favorites – weather` with these rules. The price band, hours window and caps are my suggested starting points derived from the evidence, not values tested anywhere.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mode | Paper, bankroll set to your real amount (e.g. $500) | The playbook rule: paper P&L must match real-money scale |
| Categories | Climate & Weather only (daily high/low temperature series, e.g. KXHIGH*) | Maker fee is zero; thin books; bias present |
| Price range (what you pay, either side) | **72¢–92¢** | Above 70¢ is where post-fee gains were significant; above ~92¢ the gain per win is tiny and one rules surprise wipes out ~10–19 wins |
| Never buy | Anything under 20¢ | Longshots lose in every cut of the data |
| Hours to close | **2 to 36 hours** | The bias holds at every horizon; skip the final 2 hours, when observation-watching bots pick off stale resting orders |
| Order type | Limit, **post-only** (maker), placed at the best bid or one tick above it; never cross the spread | Taker fees and the spread would eat most of a 2% edge |
| Re-pricing | Cancel and re-post unfilled orders every 30–60 min; cancel all resting orders if the price moves 5¢+ against you | Limits fills that happen only because news turned against you |
| Min edge rule | **2 percentage points**: the agent's probability, from forecasts for the settlement station (NWS/NBM, checked against the series' settlement source), must exceed the price by 2+ points, and the agent must skip if the NWS forecast clearly disagrees with the favorite | Filters out favorites the forecast says are wrong without demanding a big forecasting edge |
| Order size | 10–20 contracts per order | Keeps fee rounding negligible if you later move to fee-charging series |
| Per-market cap | $30 (about 6% of $500), **one position per city per day** | Brackets in the same city-day are one correlated bet |
| Daily caps | Max $150 deployed per day; daily loss stop 5% ($25) | From the playbook's go-live rails |
| Exit | Hold to settlement | Settlement is free; exiting early pays a second fee |
| Fees | Read `fee_type`/`fee_multiplier` per series; skip any series that gains maker fees | The schedule changed in ~50 series between July and September 2026 |

Before placing a single paper order, use the data collection you have already switched on for a free first check. Group your recorded weather prices into 70–80, 80–90 and 90–95 cent buckets, and compare each bucket's actual win rate with its average price. If 80–90 cent contracts are not winning more often than about 86% of the time, the edge is gone in 2026 and you have saved yourself a month. This checks the market's calibration directly, and it uses many more data points than your fills will.

## Runner-up: an AI forecaster with deliberately small bets

Run this second agent on paper at the same time. Its job is to answer whether your AI agent, through MCP, adds anything the market doesn't already know. The evidence says to keep it away from fast markets and to rein in its confidence. Configure `Slow-rules forecaster` with categories Economics (excluding the headline CPI, Fed and payrolls series, which are efficient and charge maker fees), Companies, Entertainment and non-sports long-tail markets. Exclude sports and 15-minute crypto. Set hours to close to **48 hours to 14 days**: AI models did better at long lead times, while 14 days limits how long a small bankroll sits locked up. Set the price range to **20¢–85¢** and the min edge to **8 percentage points**, the threshold the most-starred open-source weather bot used ([GitHub](https://github.com/suislanchez/polymarket-kalshi-weather-bot)). Instruct the agent to average its probability halfway toward the market price before it computes edge, to correct for overconfidence. Size bets in proportion to edge, about $2 per 5 points of edge with a $10 cap per market. Never use Kelly sizing, because Kelly on overconfident AI probabilities lost 99.9% in testing ([arXiv 2607.06166](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06166v1)). Use limit orders only, and require the agent to quote the market's full rules, not the summary, before it trades. In one case a 90-cent "Trump" mention market settled No because the full rules required a live broadcast ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-rules-summary-vs-full-rules)). The built-in scoreboard needs 30+ settled markets to say whether the AI's forecasts beat the market's on accuracy. Treat that as a gate, not as proof of profit.

## How to tell a real edge from luck

The hardest part of this strategy is statistical, not technical. Take a favorite bought at 85 cents that truly wins 87% of the time. It earns about +2.4% per trade, but the per-trade standard deviation is about 40%. Reaching the playbook's t-statistic of 2 then takes roughly **1,100 independent fills**, not 100. This figure is my calculation, consistent with the paper's +2.6% mean and 33% standard deviation ([Whelan PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Correlated same-day brackets make the requirement worse. In practice: run the paper agent for 4–8 weeks, check the result after removing the top three winners, and compare against the calibration check above. If it still looks positive, run 1–10 contract live orders for two weeks with approvals on. Then compare live fill rates and fill quality with paper. Measure how far prices move against you in the minutes after each fill, which tells you whether your fills come mostly from informed traders. In thin weather books Krypt Trader's "fill when the book crosses" paper logic is close to reality. In deep books like crypto it is optimistic, because it ignores the queue ahead of you ([Cryptostruct](https://cryptostruct.com/guides/kalshi-vs-polymarket-data)).

The 38 crypto presets have one good use: run them on your collected data as a **placebo**. They have essentially no edge, so however many of them look "profitable" shows you how much false success your backtester produces by chance. TurbineFi's worst strategies won 62–63% of more than 7,000 trades and still lost 75–78% ([TurbineFi](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/do-automated-trading-bots-make-money-prediction-markets-2026)). A high win rate tells you nothing when you are buying favorites. Finally, log every fill and fee yourself. Kalshi's 1099 forms do not cover trading profits, and the tax treatment of event contracts is unsettled ([Kalshi Help: Tax Info](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823849-tax-info); [Keeper Tax](https://www.keepertax.com/posts/how-to-file-taxes-on-kalshi-and-polymarket)).

## Conclusion

The realistic edge on Kalshi in 2026 is not cleverness. It is to avoid the bias that costs everyone else money (buying longshots, crossing spreads, paying fees twice) in markets too small for the professionals to bother with. That edge has a cap. At a few percent per trade on perhaps $100–150 deployed per day, a working version earns pocket money, and its main value is teaching you to measure. The evidence also has a shelf life. The Whelan sample ends in April 2025, the bias was weakest in 2025, and fees change monthly. So whether the edge still exists today is exactly the question your own collected data has to answer.

Let the order of operations decide for you. First, run the calibration check on recorded weather prices. If favorites in your data no longer win more often than their prices imply, stop there. If they do, run the paper agent until it has about a thousand fills, then trade tiny live size. Only after that look at the add-ons: the station-correct forecast model, liquidity-reward quoting, or a better AI agent. The strategies most beginners start with, 15-minute crypto and copying sportsbook lines, belong at the bottom of the list, because they are crowded, fast, or already efficiently priced.

## Addendum (Oct 8, 2026): backtest on real Kalshi data

After this report, the top recommendation was tested on 10,380 settled Kalshi temperature markets (27 US
city series, May–Oct 2026) with `tools/weather_favorites_backtest.py`.

- **Buying favorites at the ask (taker) loses** at every entry time, from −0.9% to −5.2% per $.
- **The same trade as a resting limit order**, with no maker fee on weather, about 20–24h before close,
  on **daily highs only**, returned **+2.5% to +3.6% per $ (t = 2.6–3.7)**. It held up under a strict
  fill rule. Daily lows were negative.
- Nearly all of those fills are NO orders on near-miss brackets, which fits the favorite-longshot story.

The strategy is now "Weather NO-maker". See `AGENT_SETUP.md` for the setup and the forward paper test,
and `results/` for the full tables.
